      Welcome to the Big Splash Stock Exchange

      You have $10,000 of pretend money. Use it to practise your maths with money, percentages and line graphs.

      Before you invest

      This is a maths and money lesson. There is no real money, there are no prizes and nobody wins or loses. By the end you should be able to say:

      • Maths I can read, compare and order amounts of money written as decimals.
      • Maths I can add, subtract and multiply with money to work out a total cost, including a fee.
      • Maths I can read a line graph and describe how a value changes over time.
      • Maths I can explain what a percentage such as +2% or −5% tells me.
      • Maths I can use negative numbers to describe a loss.
      • Money I can explain the difference between spending, saving and investing, and why investing has risk.
      • Computing I can search and filter information to help me make a decision.

      For parents and carers: this is a classroom activity for maths and financial education. Pupils use pretend money and made-up price changes. Nothing is bought, sold or won, and no personal information leaves this device.

      1. You start with $10,000 of pretend money.
      2. Open the Market and tap a company or fund to read what it does.
      3. Look at Performance to see how its price has moved over an hour, a day, a week or a month.
      4. Tap Buy, choose how many shares you want, then press the Buy button. Every trade costs a $5 fee.
      5. Go back to My money to watch your total, your graph and your profit or loss.
      6. Tap one of your shares and choose Sell when you want to turn it back into cash.

      Stuck on a word? The Words tab explains them.

      • Spread your money. If everything is in one company and it falls, all your money falls with it.
      • Try a fund. One share of a fund gives you a small piece of hundreds of companies.
      • Buy what you understand. Read what a company does before you buy it.
      • Do not panic. Prices wobble every minute. A small dip is normal.
      • Trade less, think more. Every trade costs $5, so lots of quick trades shrink your money.
      • Nobody can predict the next minute. Not in this game and not in real life. Be patient and judge your choices over weeks.
      • Keep a little cash. Then you can buy something new without having to sell.
